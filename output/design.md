# Campus Customs — Design Notes

A "vintage collegiate" restyle so the site reads like a real, long-running New Haven Yale
shop rather than a generic template. What changed and why it helps customers stay and buy.

## Typography
- **Fraunces** (an old-style serif with character) now sets both headings and body copy;
  **Inter** is kept only for small UI labels (buttons, chips, nav). Replaced Space Grotesk,
  which felt generic/AI-made.
- **Why:** serif body copy reads as established and trustworthy — a store with heritage, not
  a here-today dropshipper. Confidence in the brand lowers hesitation to buy.

## Color & hierarchy
- **Vintage collegiate palette:** Yale navy + warm cream paper (`#f5eede`) + aged gold, with
  warm-white product cards. Big serif headings (weight 900) set a clear hierarchy.
- **Why:** the warm, aged palette feels curated and premium; products photographed on white
  pop against the cream. Clear hierarchy guides the eye to product and price fast.

## Logos (hand-built, not AI-default, not the trademarked file)
- **Campus Customs seal:** a circular varsity monogram — interlocked serif "CC", arced
  "CAMPUS CUSTOMS · NEW HAVEN", gold rings and stars. Collegiate but indie.
- **Yale shield (footer):** a navy crest with a crafted serif "Y" and gold border, signifying
  officially-licensed status. (Custom art — Yale's actual trademarked logo is not embedded.)
- **Why:** credible, crafted marks signal a legitimate licensed retailer; the earlier flat
  "CC" box undercut trust. Legitimacy is what makes shoppers comfortable entering a card.

## Motion
- Buttons press (2px "physical" shadow), product cards lift on hover, the featured carousel
  auto-advances, Handsome Dan gently bobs, and the chat "thinking" dots bounce.
- **Why:** small, tasteful motion makes the store feel alive and responsive, which keeps
  people exploring instead of bouncing.

## Product presentation
- Warm-white cards with category eyebrow, name, short description, price, and hover lift;
  the home page opens on an auto-rotating **featured carousel** of curated items.
- **Why:** merchandise is on screen immediately and every card is one click from the detail
  page — less friction from "landing" to "buying."

## Chat feel — Handsome Dan
- The chat is now **Handsome Dan**, Yale's bulldog: a cute custom-drawn mascot on the
  launcher ("Handsome Dan here to help you look chic af") and in the header. He talks with a
  playful dog personality — the occasional "Woof!", a paw print, one dog pun per reply
  ("paws-itively classic", "fetch you a size") — while staying strictly accurate on price and
  stock. Replies **stream in** with a bouncing **dot-dot-dot** indicator while he "sniffs it out."
- **Why:** a named, on-brand mascot makes the assistant memorable and fun to talk to, which
  gets more shoppers using the single most helpful feature — and every helpful answer that
  surfaces the right product on the page is a nudge toward a purchase.
